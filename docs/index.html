The working rule of this session

What is this session?

Simulation accelerates robotics research and education by providing a safe, scalable, and cost-effective environment to test algorithms before deploying on physical hardware. This lecture and demo explore why simulation is indispensable, contrasting leading platforms like NVIDIA Isaac Sim, mjlab, and Unity to guide engine selection for specific research and classroom needs. A live demonstration highlights physics fidelity, sensor emulation, and rapid prototyping capabilities. The session concludes with an interactive Q&A and curated resource sharing for immediate implementation.

Session goals

  • Explain the critical role of simulation in modern robotics development, testing, and education.
  • Compare industry-standard simulation platforms to help educators and researchers choose the right workflow.
  • Showcase a live (digital) robot simulation to illustrate real-time physics, perception pipeline integration, and environment interaction.
  • Provide accessible learning resources and lead an open discussion to support practical adoption.

Students will be able to

  • Evaluate simulation frameworks (e.g., Isaac Sim, Unity, etc) based on physics realism, GPU acceleration, and sensor fidelity.
  • Identify key advantages and constraints of running robotics algorithms in simulated environments versus physical hardware.
  • Demonstrate basic robot control and sensor visualization within a real-time simulation engine.
  • Select appropriate simulation tools tailored for specific coursework or advanced research projects.

The live demo

One small rover in a 6 × 6 m studio, carrying wheel encoders, an IMU, a bumper, a 15-beam lidar and an RGB-D camera. The whole robot is one readable XML file, and everything runs on a laptop with MuJoCo. These are the recorded versions; in class they run live.

1. Physics playground

Foam, rubber and steel: same shapes, different materials. Only rubber bounces; only steel slides. Change one number, re-run in seconds.

python demo/01_physics_playground.py

2. Drive the robot

Arrow-key driving, then an autopilot aimed at the goal. With no sensors it drives straight into the blue box.

python demo/02_drive_the_robot.py

3. Sensor dashboard

The same robot, sensing. Lidar builds a map, A* plans a route, pure pursuit follows it. Camera, depth, lidar, IMU and wheel encoders update live.

python demo/03_sensor_dashboard.py

4. Camera perception

Camera only: RGB to HSV, threshold the orange beacon, find the blob, steer toward it. Search, track, arrive.

python demo/04_camera_perception.py

5. The sim-to-real gap

Twelve randomized "realities": different floor and tire friction, mass, motor strength, sensor noise and 30 ms of delay.

Replaying commands that were perfect in the ideal simulator reaches the goal in 2 of 12 worlds. Sensing and re-planning reaches it in 11 of 12.

python demo/05_sim_to_real_gap.py

Choosing a platform

Three platforms, judged on the three criteria that matter most for robotics: physics realism, GPU acceleration and sensor fidelity. Checked October 2026.

NVIDIA Isaac Sim + Isaac Labmjlab (MuJoCo + MuJoCo Warp)Unity + Robotics Hub
Physics realismPhysX 5 articulations, Newton backend in 6.0. Strong for rigid bodies and grasping.MuJoCo, the reference for accurate, stable contact in control and learning research.PhysX ArticulationBody, tuned for games. Fine for interaction, weaker for precise contact.
GPU accelerationGPU physics; thousands of parallel environments in Isaac Lab.MuJoCo Warp; thousands of parallel environments on an NVIDIA GPU.Graphics on the GPU, physics on the CPU. Smaller-scale training with ML-Agents.
Sensor fidelityHighest: ray-traced cameras, lidar and radar, labelled synthetic data.Rasterised RGB-D, ray-cast rangefinders, IMU, touch, force.High-quality cameras; other sensors through packages or scripts.
Runs onRTX GPU with 16 GB+, Ubuntu or Windows 11. No macOS.MuJoCo on any laptop. Training with mjlab needs an NVIDIA GPU.Any recent laptop, including Macs.
Best forSynthetic data, digital twins, photoreal sensors. OpenUSD fits film and VFX pipelines.Locomotion, manipulation, learning; teaching dynamics.Interaction, VR/AR, installations, game-dev students.

Use Unity when the experience matters most, MuJoCo or mjlab when the physics and learning matter most, and Isaac Sim when the sensors and photorealism matter most. Expect to combine them.

Also covered: Gazebo for ROS 2 robot stacks, Webots for first courses, and Newton, the new open GPU physics engine. Full comparison with sources.
